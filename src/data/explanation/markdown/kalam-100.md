# Salaam-e Raza

A salaam poem by Ala Hazrat (Imam Ahmed Raza Khan). Every couplet ends with *"Laakhoñ Salaam"* — millions of salutations.

The reciter explains each couplet's difficult Arabic and Urdu words, then gives the context behind them.

## The Poem's Journey

The poem salutes people in order of closeness to the Prophet ﷺ:

**The Prophet ﷺ** — The first 96 couplets describe him: his face brighter than the moon, his voice the key to "Kun" (Be), his hair fragrant as musk, his fingers fountains of light. He was content with barley bread even though he ruled two worlds.

**Hasan (RA)** — "The one who rode on the Prophet's shoulders." A companion once joked about this. The Prophet replied: *"How lovely even the rider is."*

**Husayn (RA)** — The martyr of Karbala. He offered his last prayer in prostration, wearing a blood-red robe. Ala Hazrat wrote elsewhere: "By the sacrifice of those two, on Judgment Day, make Raza bloom like a flower."

**The Wives** — Called "mothers of the believers" — not a metaphor, the reciter stresses, but a Quranic ruling. Aisha (RA) was the only wife who came to the Prophet as a virgin and grew up in Islam from birth.

**The Companions** — Abu Bakr (the truthful), Umar (the sword of truth), Uthman (who gathered the Quran's scattered pearls into one book), Ali (the lion of God who split Khaibar's gate).

**The Saints** — Abdul Qadir Jilani and other Sufi saints, proof that the Prophet's light travels through time.

**Ala Hazrat's family** — His own father, teachers, and descendants. The final couplet is his plea: *"When the Gathering comes, tell Mustafa that RAZA served."*

## The Reciter's Method

He recites a couplet, breaks down each difficult word, tells the story behind it, and repeats it so listeners absorb the meaning. Warm, conversational — like a teacher who genuinely wants you to understand.
